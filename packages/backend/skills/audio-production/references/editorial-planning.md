# Editorial planning

Establish show identity,audience need,episode question,format,speaker roles,source basis and intended action. Distinguish interview,solo explanation,reported narrative,fiction and synthetic conversation. Inspect actual show conventions,approved voice,sponsor terms and recordings before proposing another style.

Build segments with purpose,evidence,questions/transitions and a realistic time budget. Include context,development and a useful ending where appropriate without fixed minute allocations. Do not pad a short answer into an episode or invent a dramatic story to support a factual claim.

For interviews prepare specific open questions and follow-up paths from actual research. Anticipated answers are not quotations. Identify recording/usage consent,off-record constraints and sensitive subjects. Guest outreach,booking and sending questions require applicable messaging authority;preparing the packet does not contact anyone.

For reported material track quote/source identity,timecodes and fact checks. Preserve meaning during edits;do not splice a speaker into a proposition they did not make. Music/archive rights,credits and sponsorship/synthetic disclosure need actual review for the placement and jurisdiction.

Deliver a runnable segment plan with input/owner,cues,estimated timing and missing evidence. Actual read-through/recorded duration is a later observation. Reuse scriptwriting for full dialogue/narration rather than duplicating it here.
