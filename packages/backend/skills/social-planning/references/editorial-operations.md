# Editorial operations

## Intake and capacity

A plan must fit people, assets and review latency. Identify who writes, designs, reviews and can publish. Account for translations, legal/product review when applicable, and time needed to collect real examples. If the requested cadence exceeds capacity, show the tradeoff between fewer high-quality items, simpler formats and delayed dates. Do not invent additional staff or available automation.

For each theme, state the audience question, credible source and useful takeaway. An existing long-form article can feed several adaptations when each has a distinct purpose; simple copy duplication across channels adds maintenance without new value. Preserve the source link and check whether its claims remain current.

## Calendar identity and concurrency

Use an existing row ID where available; otherwise create a stable ID once. On reruns, match the intended campaign/item before appending. Record the source revision you read. If another editor changed a row, merge the specific proposed change after rereading or surface the conflict. Never resolve a conflict by replacing the entire calendar with a locally older copy.

Suggested transitions: idea → briefed → draft → in review → approved → scheduled → published. Add rejected, failed, cancelled and uncertain as needed. A task can move back to draft when substantive changes occur. Preserve the approval target revision and publication history even when the current version changes. A new date may require a new review when offer validity, events or current claims are affected.

## Production packets

A packet should identify the item, target channel/account, objective, audience, approved claim sources, final copy, CTA target, asset paths, crop requirements and accessibility text. List rights/consent restrictions and date-sensitive conditions. A video brief needs a hook, scene sequence, narration/captions, asset sources, desired duration and export requirements; a still-image brief needs message hierarchy, text and visual constraints. Do not fill unknown product details with plausible specifics.

Inspect the actual exported asset. A design URL alone may point to an editable source that the publisher cannot access. Verify that links resolve for the intended recipient and that exports are complete. Avoid private access tokens in shared URLs or filenames. If export is unavailable, label the deliverable as a source design and explain the remaining operation.

## Channel adaptation

Adapt structure and density rather than just cropping or truncating. Check current official constraints for the platform being used. A caption that relies on a clickable link may fail on a surface where links are plain text. Ensure captions and alt text convey essential information without stuffing promotional keywords. Where a platform does not support the intended feature, propose a feasible alternative without claiming the feature exists.

## Ongoing operation

At each cycle, inspect unfinished work, upcoming deadlines, approvals, publication receipts and actual results. Carry forward relevant unfinished items by ID; explicitly retire stale items. Keep the calendar usable for the human operator, with a clear next action and owner. Preserve brand separation even when a team or tool account handles several brands.
