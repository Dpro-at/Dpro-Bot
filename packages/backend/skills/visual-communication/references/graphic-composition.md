# Graphic composition

## Content and hierarchy

Identify the essential message, support and next action. Use actual approved content early enough to expose line lengths, overflow and visual competition. Do not invent product claims or cut material conditions merely to fit an elegant layout. If the requested density does not fit, show the concrete tradeoff between hierarchy, length, format and output size.

Build reading order with scale, weight, space, alignment and contrast. A grid should support the content rather than force every element into arbitrary symmetry. Check relationships and whitespace at the intended size, including the smallest required adaptation. A headline that works in a large artboard may become unreadable in a feed crop.

## Typography and language

Use approved/available type and verify the relevant license, glyph coverage and export behavior. Inspect numerals, punctuation, long names and required scripts. Do not replace important text with an image-only treatment without considering the recipient's needs. Outlined text can aid a particular export but reduces editability/searchability; retain an appropriate source.

## Color and imagery

Define foreground/background roles and actual pairings. A brand accent is not automatically a safe text color on every surface. A contrast calculation answers a narrow numeric question; inspect real gradients, image backgrounds and scale separately. Keep semantic distinctions legible without relying on color alone when relevant.

Select imagery by message, rights, crop and technical quality. Do not distort a product or source photo to make a crop fit. Preserve essential context and factual accuracy. Use an approved alternative when the source cannot support the intended format, or expose the missing asset.

## Adaptations

Keep the recognizable message hierarchy while adapting composition to aspect ratio, channel and density. A mechanical crop is not always an adequate adaptation. Check safe regions, baseline alignment, logo use and the full recipient context. Distinguish a screen preview from verified print/color output; production conditions may require separate checks.
