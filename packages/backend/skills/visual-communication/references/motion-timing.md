# Motion, timing and continuity

## Purpose and medium

Identify the movement's job: explain a change, guide attention, provide feedback, connect states or tell the requested story. Omit motion that adds distraction without serving the use. A frequent keyboard action may need immediate response; an editorial sequence may allow a longer reveal. Do not impose one duration/easing table on every context.

Distinguish fixed-duration video, looping media and interactive animation. Fixed timelines need explicit timebase/frame boundaries and final duration. Interactive transitions need interruption, reversal, repeated activation, focus and state handling. A static keyframe cannot establish either behavior.

## Choreography

Design a clear beginning, action and resolution where useful. Coordinate what the viewer should notice first and preserve object/state continuity. Use secondary motion only when it supports the message; an ambient layer is not mandatory for every piece. Avoid piling effects onto a scene to compensate for unclear structure.

Choose easing from movement purpose, material metaphor and interaction behavior, then inspect the actual result. A linear or opacity-only change can be appropriate in some contexts; treat recipes as choices, not universal prohibitions. Check overshoot, cut boundaries, readable holds, entrance/exit relationships and loop seams.

## Time and audio

Use a declared rational frame rate and integer frame intervals for deterministic timeline planning. Explain whether interval ends are exclusive. Verify the actual export's timebase/duration separately; a valid manifest is not a media probe. Audio timing, captions and voice pacing need their own readback/preview.

Reserve readable hold time for required text at the real size and context. A fast animation that technically shows a label may still fail communication. Inspect the entire sequence and key transitions, not only selected stills.

## Accessibility and alternatives

Honor relevant reduced-motion preferences and provide an equivalent non-motion route for essential information when needed. Assess repetitive/flashing behavior against the applicable current requirements when such effects are used. Do not assume a slower or dimmer effect is automatically acceptable. A numeric manifest cannot certify these properties.

## Production and handoff

Storyboard/animatic first when it can prevent expensive rework. Record dimensions, timebase, duration, assets, rights, audio/caption requirements and output format. Distinguish planned timing, source project, rendered preview and final export. Retrieve actual outputs and inspect them before reporting delivery.
