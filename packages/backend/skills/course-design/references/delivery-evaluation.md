# Delivery and evaluation

Define the delivered state: design specification, complete source materials, locally packaged artifact, learning-platform preview, published course or learner-tested offering. These are separate claims. A ZIP that opens is not proof that a learning platform tracks completion or that learners acquired the skill.

## Review

Check domain accuracy, outcomes/practice/assessment meaning, learner instructions, expected resources, accessibility, estimated effort and support. Inspect the actual materials and rubric rather than accepting a filled alignment table. Verify referenced sources and asset rights within the task scope.

For an existing course, compare required changes to the original and preserve approved content/policies. Keep a revision record and validate meaningful links after moving files. Avoid silently replacing unknown instructor dates, grading rules or contact information with plausible fiction.

## Pilot

Plan a small representative trial when it can resolve material uncertainty. Define participant context, permissions, task, data handling, observation method and success criterion. Keep assistance visible: a facilitator explaining every instruction changes what the pilot demonstrates. Do not recruit/contact people or collect learner records without the applicable authorization.

Record observed completion, misunderstandings, time/effort and assessed performance with relevant denominators. Separate designer prediction from learner evidence. A few successful participants can reveal usability issues but do not establish a universal effect size or certify learning quality.

## Target platform

When export/import is requested, inspect the current platform and supported format/version. Packaging standards, navigation and tracking must be tested in the actual authorized environment. Keep credentials out of shared materials. Do not enroll learners, overwrite a live course, start paid integrations or publish to obtain a screenshot.

## Improve

Use evidence to choose the next correction: missing prerequisite, confusing instructions, insufficient practice, bad scoring criterion or a platform fault. Do not lengthen content automatically after every failed item. Keep unresolved issues with a concrete acceptance step and owner rather than converting them into a generic pass.

Report authored/reviewed/piloted/published states separately, including actual commands, participants or platform only when those actions occurred. Preserve private learner responses; a portable settings/template package should not contain them by default.
