# Channel playbooks

Choose the section that matches the requested deliverable. These are editorial options, not fixed word counts or claims about algorithmic performance.

## Explanatory article

Start from the reader's search or learning need. State what the article will help them understand or do. Develop a sequence in which each section answers a necessary question, explains a distinction or demonstrates a step. Use examples where they make the idea testable.

An article should not postpone its useful answer until after a generic introduction. Include context where it changes the decision, with sources for important facts. Close with the appropriate next action or implication; do not repeat every heading as a second summary.

When requested, provide title, proposed slug and a concise description consistent with the actual article. Verify material platform/SEO requirements rather than asserting fixed display lengths. Internal and external links should help the reader, not meet a quota.

## Social post

Pick one idea the audience can absorb in the chosen format. Make the opening specific enough that the next lines have a clear purpose. Develop a short argument, practical lesson, example or observation, then give the reader a useful ending.

An anecdote must be real or explicitly fictional. Do not invent revenue, hours saved or personal struggle to make the story relatable. Format for the named platform after checking relevant current constraints. Hashtags, questions and calls for comments are optional editorial choices, not mandatory endings.

When adapting a long article, preserve its claim boundaries. A shorter post must not turn a cautious finding into a universal promise. Put visual suggestions and source notes outside the publishable copy.

## Email or newsletter

Define why the recipient would welcome this message. The subject and preview should work together without making contradictory promises. Lead with the useful information; use sections only when the newsletter contains genuinely separate items.

Choose an action appropriate to the relationship and purpose. A newsletter may invite reading; a service notice may simply explain a change. Do not manufacture urgency or imply personalization based on data that was never provided.

Prepare subject alternatives with different editorial angles when asked. Check links, sender identity and any required unsubscribe or company details through the actual sending context. Drafting does not establish a lawful mailing basis or authorize sending.

## Landing or product page

Organize the page around the visitor's decision: relevance, capability, proof, important questions and the next step. Use real product behavior and accurate availability. Explain features in terms of work the visitor can perform without promising outcomes the product cannot establish.

Testimonials, logos and numbers require authorized evidence. If none exists, use a demonstration or concrete capability description instead of fictional proof. An FAQ should address real uncertainty, not introduce unsupported objections just to fill a section.

Separate copy from implementation notes. Identify necessary screenshots, interactions and asset rights. Keep the primary action consistent with what the destination actually does.

## Announcement

Identify the actual news, who it affects, what changed, when it applies and where to learn more. Distinguish a released feature from a roadmap item. Preserve limitations that affect eligibility or availability.

Use attributable quotes only when provided and authorized. Do not create a media contact or an executive statement. Keep background context subordinate to the change itself.

## Customer story

Confirm permission to identify the customer and use its outcomes. Explain the initial situation, intervention and observed result, including the period and measurement basis. Distinguish reported benefit from verified causal impact.

If outcome data is missing, draft an implementation story with the appropriate limitation. Never fill a missing metric or testimonial with an invented success claim. A fictional case should be clearly marked and should not appear to be customer evidence.

## Repurposing

Preserve the evidence and brand boundaries while redesigning the structure for the next channel. Record any change in emphasis that could overstate the original. A repurposed post should stand alone; it should not assume the reader saw an earlier campaign.
