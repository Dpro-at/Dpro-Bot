# Claims, voice and editorial judgment

## Evidence categories

- Supported claim: identify the source and verify that it supports the wording, scope and date.
- Inference: state the underlying facts and avoid presenting the inference as a measured result.
- Fictional example: label it; do not put it in a testimonial, customer logo or factual-results section.
- Unresolved claim: remove it from ready-to-publish copy or preserve an unmistakable review marker in a draft.

A source URL alone is not proof. Check the passage, methodology and whether the source owns the claim. A product description does not prove a customer's return on investment.

## Numbers and promises

Retain units, denominators and measurement windows. Distinguish typical, possible and observed results. A percent improvement is not a percentage-point change. Avoid universal guarantees when evidence covers only a limited scenario.

Prices, dates, plan limits and availability change. Check the current authoritative record when the copy depends on them. If the task is historical, use the applicable historical version.

## Brand separation

Use the specified brand's authorized voice, facts and assets. Do not mix one brand's testimonials, language or customer claims into another's copy. Where the brand forbids public competitor mentions, review the entire draft and visual suggestions for those names.

A voice guide supplies style, not a license to invent experiences. A testimonial voice must remain attributable to its real author. Preserve technical terms needed by the audience while explaining uncommon ones.

## Editorial questions

Can the intended reader tell what this piece offers? Does each section add useful information? Does the opening deliver what it promises? Is the action real and appropriate? Would a skeptical reader understand the evidence and its limits?

A polished draft with an unsupported decisive claim needs revision. A mechanically clean claim map is useful review input, not final editorial approval.
