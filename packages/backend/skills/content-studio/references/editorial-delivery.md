# Editorial delivery

## Agree the edit

Identify the draft/version, audience, purpose, publication format, language variant, house style and requested depth. Developmental editing addresses argument, sequence and coverage; line editing addresses expression and rhythm; copyediting addresses correctness and consistency; proofreading checks the near-final presentation. Do not silently turn a proofread into a wholesale rewrite.

Read the complete supplied material before drawing structural conclusions. If only an excerpt is available, scope the review accordingly. Identify protected elements: quotations, code, citations, data, legal wording, intentional character voice and terminology. Preserve the original and use the requested tracked-change, patch, revised-copy or comment format. Resolve the most important missing constraint while continuing useful review.

## Edit in purposeful passes

First assess whether the structure serves the stated purpose: clear premise, relevant evidence, logical progression, proportionate detail and a useful ending. Identify unsupported transitions, repetition and missing reader context. Explain structural moves by their effect on the reader rather than invoking a formula every document must follow.

Then improve sentences and paragraphs while preserving certainty, agency, chronology and attribution. Replace vague phrasing with supported specifics, reduce repetition and clarify references. Keep useful technical terms and intentional rhythm. Passive voice can be appropriate when the actor is unknown or irrelevant; punctuation, long words and balanced structures are not automatic defects or evidence of machine authorship.

Check names, dates, units, internal terminology, capitalization, grammar, references and cross-references against supplied evidence and chosen style. Do not invent citations or silently strengthen claims. Source verification is a separate evidence task: identify claims needing it and use the existing research workflow if available. A polished sentence can still be false. Keep quotations accurate and distinguish a proposed paraphrase from a verified quotation.

## Preserve voice and output integrity

Match the author's purpose and agreed voice without flattening humor, dialect, cultural expression or technical precision. Apply typography conventions appropriate to the target: curly punctuation is not universally required in plain text, source code or data. Preserve literal strings, links and syntax. Check that revisions have not broken Markdown, tables, headings, citations or accessibility-relevant labels.

For substantive changes record original location, revised wording or move, reason, meaning impact and unresolved query. Separate necessary corrections from optional taste. If an inconsistency cannot be resolved from evidence, retain an explicit question rather than quietly choosing a plausible fact. Work within agreed length without removing a material qualification or required disclosure.

## Complete the handoff

Deliver the revised artifact or requested markup, a short account of major changes and a prioritized list of unresolved author/source questions. Check the requested scope against the original, including numbers, negations, modal verbs, names and attribution. A final proof should inspect the rendered destination when available; a source-only check cannot establish pagination, font, line-break or visual quality. Editing does not authorize publication, external uploads or overwriting unrelated drafts.
