# Conversion copy without invented proof

## Establish the job of the copy

Determine whether this is a new page, a rewrite, a constrained edit or a set of alternatives. Preserve the requested scope and existing brand voice. For a narrow headline edit, do not replace the complete page strategy. Identify the audience's immediate need, desired action, offer, real differentiator and strongest available proof. Read existing product/voice context before asking the user to repeat it.

## Build the argument

Connect the audience's problem to an actual mechanism, supported benefit and appropriate next action. Distinguish a product capability from a customer outcome; a capability does not guarantee time savings or revenue. Use concrete product facts and genuine customer language when available. Do not invent quotations, benchmark percentages, awards, guarantees, scarcity or customer counts to make the page sound persuasive.

A home page should orient the intended reader and route the next step; a landing page should support a specific campaign promise; pricing should explain actual terms and choice; a feature page should show the mechanism and relevant evidence. Match the structure to the task instead of forcing every page through the same formula.

## Draft usable units

Write a clear headline, supporting statement, evidence, objection handling and CTA where they serve the actual page. Alternatives should vary a meaningful argument or emphasis; cosmetic synonym swaps offer little choice. Tie the CTA to what really happens next, such as opening a form, starting a trial or requesting a call. Include material conditions rather than burying them behind an overly broad promise.

When proof is missing, use a supported qualitative claim or leave a precise request for evidence. Do not turn an internal placeholder into production copy. Keep the claim ledger from the parent workflow and carry each factual proof source through revisions.

## Preserve voice and edit deliberately

Read actual approved samples and retain their distinctive level of directness, vocabulary and rhythm. Remove filler and abstract superlatives when they obscure the product, but do not replace every voice with a rigid list of banned words. Use conversational transitions that fit the argument. Avoid adversarial pressure or emotional manipulation when a direct explanation would support the decision.

For an edit, show the resulting copy and explain significant changes only when useful. For alternatives, state the intended audience/angle and proof dependency. Do not claim that one will convert better without testing evidence.

## Review and measurement

Check factual support, offer terms, CTA destination, consistency across the page, readability and accessibility of linked controls. Evaluate the complete page experience when possible; strong copy can fail if the form or next step is broken. Propose a bounded experiment with a defined conversion event, comparison and observation window if performance is part of the task. A simulated preference judgment is not an actual conversion test.

Acceptance cases: an unsupported numerical promise becomes qualified copy or an evidence request; a narrow edit preserves the rest of the page; an existing distinctive voice survives the rewrite; pricing copy includes material conditions; CTA text matches the actual destination. Keep package validation separate from real model/task results.
