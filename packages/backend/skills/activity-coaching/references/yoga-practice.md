# General yoga practice planning

## Understand the practice request

Identify the person's purpose,experience,available time,space,props and preferences. They may want a gentle movement break,a beginner sequence or support organizing an existing practice. Distinguish general adult wellness practice from treatment,rehabilitation,advanced skill coaching or spiritual instruction. Respect cultural context and the person's preferences without claiming a single universal yoga tradition.

Apply the shared coaching boundary and ask about reported restrictions relevant to the requested movements. Do not infer that flexibility or prior class attendance establishes suitability for every pose. New pain,injury,balance problems or conditions affecting practice may require individual qualified guidance. Pregnancy/postpartum and clinical adaptations are beyond a generic routine adult sequence.

## Design a coherent sequence

Choose a small number of accessible positions and transitions aligned with the goal. Explain starting position,movement direction,support options,approximate duration or repetitions and how to exit. Account for getting to and from the floor,weight-bearing demands and available stable supports. A chair-based alternative should avoid assuming that the person can kneel,stand unsupported or balance on one leg.

Build gradually from comfortable preparation to the main practice and an unhurried finish. Avoid chaining unfamiliar transitions just to make the routine appear advanced. Give alternatives that preserve the session's purpose while reducing demand. A pose's traditional name can be included when useful,but plain-language directions must make the task understandable.

Use comfortable breathing without forced breath retention or hyperventilation as routine defaults. The person can keep eyes open,skip a breathing exercise or choose ordinary quiet rest. Do not promise detoxification,trauma release,medical treatment or a particular emotional response. A movement plan should not pressure the person into disclosure or unwanted spiritual language.

## Keep demand appropriate

A beginner sequence should not default to unsupported inversions,extreme end-range positions,forceful breathing or heated practice. These carry context-specific demands and may need qualified instruction or medical guidance. Do not imply that a text plan substitutes for supervision or that a pose is safe for everyone if performed slowly.

Encourage comfortable controllable movement and an easy exit. If a position provokes pain,dizziness,numbness or other concerning symptoms,stop and follow appropriate qualified guidance rather than pushing through. A numeric pain threshold is not clearance. Do not diagnose a tight muscle,misalignment or blocked energy from a brief report.

Adapt based on actual response,not a required progression toward deeper poses. Practice can improve through clarity,comfort and consistency without adding range or difficulty. A calmer feeling after one session is a self-reported experience,not proof of treatment effectiveness. Avoid claims of guaranteed sleep,anxiety or pain outcomes.

## Deliver and follow through

Provide the sequence with timing,transitions,props,alternatives and stop/modification cues. Identify the intended audience and assumptions,and name missing physical observation where relevant. A search link is not a verified demonstration;if recommending a video,check that the actual content matches the described practice and level.

Invite a brief optional review of comfort,enjoyment and practical fit. No personal health diary,device sync,class purchase or recurring message follows automatically. Distinguish the plan from a completed session and real clinical clearance. Actual safety,effective technique and benefits remain outside what preparing this artifact can establish.
