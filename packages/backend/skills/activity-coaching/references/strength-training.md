# Strength training planning

## Define the purpose and baseline

Identify whether the person wants general strength,functional capacity,hypertrophy support or help following an existing program. Clarify experience,recent sessions,available equipment,session time and preferred units. Use relevant current recovery and reported restrictions. A previous personal record is not automatically an appropriate working load today,and a beginner does not need a maximal test to receive useful guidance.

Apply the shared coaching boundary before programming. Do not diagnose pain,interpret laboratory results or give rehabilitation/return-to-lifting clearance. If safe suitability depends on an unresolved condition,avoid the affected or demanding prescription and direct the person to qualified guidance. Physical technique cannot be certified from a text description.

## Choose a feasible structure

Select movement patterns and exercises that serve the goal and fit actual equipment and skill. Consider knee/hip-dominant work,pushing,pulling and appropriate trunk/control work without requiring every pattern in every session. Exercise names alone do not guarantee equivalence:changing a barbell movement to a band or supported option changes stability,resistance and skill demands.

Specify preparation,exercise order,working sets,repetition range,effort guidance,rest and an accessible substitution. Count setup and recovery in the session duration. Explain one or two useful technique cues tied to control and comfort;avoid a long list of rigid universal body positions. When demonstration or in-person feedback would materially help,recommend a qualified instructor rather than claiming the plan verifies form.

Choose conservative initial demand from actual experience and the person's ability to complete controlled repetitions. If load is unknown,use a self-adjusted effort cue with room to stop before technique deteriorates. Do not invent exact kilograms or a percentage of an unmeasured maximum. Clearly identify warm-up versus working sets and whether a prescribed number is a starting suggestion or an observed prior result.

## Progress from comparable evidence

Review the same movement/version,units,sets,repetitions,load and reported effort across comparable sessions. An estimated one-repetition maximum is an estimate affected by method and conditions;it is not permission to attempt that load. Total tonnage can describe work but does not fully measure stimulus,quality or recovery.

When the planned work is consistently manageable with suitable control and recovery,consider a modest change in one relevant variable. Repeating the same prescription,using an easier variation or reducing demand can be the correct decision. Avoid mandatory personal records,automatic weekly load increases or a universal ten-percent progression rule. A stall can reflect equipment limits,measurement noise,skill,fatigue or changing goals;do not diagnose overtraining from two sessions.

Plan recovery around the whole week and actual response,including other demanding activity. Do not prescribe compensatory extra sessions after a missed workout. Persistent pain,unusual symptoms or deteriorating function require reassessment and appropriate qualified care,not merely a new exercise variation. Avoid routine maximal/failure lifting or complex high-skill lifts without the necessary context and supervision.

## Deliver and review

Provide a usable session or weekly plan with rationale,assumptions,substitutions and a clear rule for the next review. Log planned versus actually completed work and note any changed exercise,unit or effort scale. Keep health notes private under actual host permissions. Do not publish records,sync a device,buy equipment or create reminders automatically.

State what remains unknown:actual movement quality,physical response,clinical suitability and longer-term progress. A written program does not establish strength gain,injury prevention or safe execution. The person retains the option to stop,modify or seek in-person instruction.
