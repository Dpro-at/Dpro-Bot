# Comfortable mobility planning

## Understand the intended movement

Identify the everyday or training task the person wants to perform more comfortably,and current experience,equipment,time and relevant restrictions. Distinguish general range-of-motion practice from injury rehabilitation,diagnosis or treatment. Do not infer a structural defect or corrective need from a posture description or single photograph.

Check whether the person reports pain,new limitation,instability,numbness,weakness or a recent injury. Follow the shared coaching boundary;do not prescribe a painful stretch as a diagnostic test or advise forcing a joint through symptoms. A clinician's specific restriction takes precedence over a generic mobility sequence.

## Build a simple sequence

Choose a small set of comfortable movements relevant to the stated task,with clear setup,direction,range/effort cue,duration or repetitions and support options. Use stable surroundings and accessible positions. Distinguish gentle active movement,static stretching and loaded mobility;they have different demands and are not interchangeable.

Start within controllable comfortable range and avoid bouncing,forced end range or aggressive partner pressure as routine defaults. Explain the purpose without claiming to realign bones,release toxins or cure pain. Flexibility and control vary between people;more range is not always the useful goal.

## Observe and adapt

Ask for the person's actual response during and after the sequence. Reduce range,change position or stop a provoking movement. Do not use a fixed pain score as permission to continue or treat temporary ease as proof of healing. New or persistent concerning symptoms need qualified assessment.

Progress through comfortable consistency and relevant task control before adding demand. A small repeatable sequence can be sufficient;do not add complexity merely to appear personalized. If visual technique assessment is unavailable,provide clear cues and name that limit rather than claiming form was verified.

## Deliver

Provide the sequence,an easier alternative,stop/adaptation cues and a review point tied to the person's task. Keep outcomes framed as reported comfort/control,not guaranteed pain relief or injury prevention. Actual physical performance and clinical suitability remain unverified by a written plan. Do not create ongoing symptom monitoring or save health notes without the host's actual authority.
