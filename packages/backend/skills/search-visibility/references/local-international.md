# Local and international contexts

## Local

Establish the real business entity, served locations, actual contact details and whether customers visit a physical location or the business travels to them. Verify current profile eligibility with the platform's official policy before proposing creation or edits. Do not fabricate location pages, reviews, business categories or opening hours.

Separate profile access/verification problems from website indexing and local market competition. Compare consistent identity details across legitimate sources, while distinguishing formatting variations from conflicting phone/address information. Location pages should contain useful, accurate local information instead of a city-name substitution template.

Review acquisition and response practices for reviews against current platform rules. Do not imply incentives, invented reviews or selective pressure are valid optimization. Report observed data coverage: an internal dashboard export is not every local search impression.

## International

List the actual languages/regions, URL patterns and intended fallback. Inspect main-content translation rather than only interface labels. Evaluate whether each locale has enough useful material and operational support to justify a distinct page.

Check alternate declarations against the current official syntax and intended region/language combinations. Inspect self/reciprocal references, valid reachable targets and consistency across HTML, headers or sitemap methods actually used. Cross-locale canonical choices may conflict with the intent to index alternates; diagnose the desired architecture before changing them.

Do not propose automatic geographic redirects that hide content from users or crawlers without evaluating accessibility and discovery. Missing an optional fallback alone is not proof of invalid markup. Record sample coverage and unresolved locale pairs instead of certifying a whole multilingual site from its home page.
