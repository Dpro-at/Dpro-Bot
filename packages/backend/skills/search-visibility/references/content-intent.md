# Content, intent and prioritization

## Query evidence

Preserve the source and date of query data, including country, device, branded/non-branded segmentation and any suppressed or incomplete rows. Do not invent volume from language-model familiarity. Public search results can suggest intent for the checked locale/time, but are a changing observation rather than the user's complete query distribution.

Map intent to a page that can actually satisfy it. Informational, comparison, transactional and navigational needs often require different material. A page should answer the relevant question and provide a truthful next step. Do not stretch an unrelated page to include every possible keyword.

## Titles, snippets and headings

Assess whether the title accurately identifies the page and differentiates it in context. Check repeated templates where they erase useful distinctions. A search engine may rewrite a title or choose a snippet; document that behavior separately from the submitted metadata. Character counts can help editing, but visible truncation varies and is not a universal ranking penalty.

Headings should organize content for readers and assistive technology. Investigate genuine missing structure and confusing hierarchy. Avoid claiming an automatic SEO failure from multiple H1 elements without considering the actual document. Descriptions should faithfully summarize the page rather than promise unsupported benefits.

## Content quality and links

Check accuracy, evidence, originality, ownership and the reader's ability to complete the task. Page length alone cannot establish quality. A concise answer can be sufficient, while a long page may remain repetitive or unsupported. Identify stale product facts and broken examples as concrete defects.

Review internal links as discovery and navigation paths. Distinguish an actually orphaned URL from one missing only in a limited crawler sample. Use descriptive anchors that fit the reader's task. For media, meaningful alt text depends on purpose; decorative images can correctly have empty alt text.

## Overlap and consolidation

Pages sharing keywords do not automatically cannibalize each other. Examine intent, query/page performance, canonical behavior and the unique value of each. If consolidation is justified, map old URLs and distinct content to the retained destination, preserve relevant links and define verification. Redirect changes require explicit implementation scope and a rollback plan appropriate to the site.

## Priority and experiments

Separate evidence-backed repairs from growth hypotheses. A broken canonical affecting product pages may deserve priority over optional title experiments; impact also depends on actual business scope. Record confidence and dependencies. Avoid false precision such as a promised 32% lift without a defensible model.

A title or content experiment should state the target cohort, hypothesis, change, baseline, observation window and confounders. Search outcomes can lag and vary; do not declare causation from a short before/after chart. Keep a change log so later results can be interpreted.
