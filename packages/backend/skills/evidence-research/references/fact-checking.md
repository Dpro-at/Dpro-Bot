# Claim-by-claim fact checking

## Define the proposition

Preserve the exact statement, speaker/publisher, date and relevant context. Split compound claims when one part can be true and another unsupported. Identify population, geography, units, time window and the implied comparison. A rhetorical opinion is not a factual proposition unless it contains an independently checkable assertion. State the checkable interpretation rather than silently rewriting the claim to something easier to verify.

## Trace evidence to its origin

Find the controlling record or original evidence for each proposition. A company announcement establishes what the company announced; it may not establish measured effectiveness. A law's issuing text establishes its wording, while application to a specific case may require additional context. Several publications repeating one press release remain one evidence origin.

Independent corroboration matters when the evidence is contested or empirical, but a rigid two-source rule can obscure a single authoritative record that directly settles a narrow claim. Explain what each source establishes and what it cannot. If the controlling source is inaccessible, record that gap rather than citing a summary as if it were inspected.

## Numbers and media

Recalculate arithmetic from the stated inputs when possible. Check denominator, baseline, absolute versus relative change and observed versus projected values. Retain the relevant date/version. Do not treat rounded figures as precise data or combine incompatible populations. For images/video, distinguish verifying provenance/context from proving that every depicted event occurred as described; missing metadata alone does not establish fabrication.

## Verdicts with reasons

Use clear supported, contradicted, misleading/context missing, mixed or unresolved findings as appropriate to the task. Explain the decisive evidence and scope rather than assigning arbitrary certainty percentages. Lack of evidence does not establish falsehood. A partly correct numerical claim can still be misleading if its omitted denominator changes the conclusion.

Present each decisive factual conclusion with a direct inspected citation. Keep observations, interpretation and proposed correction separate. Offer a corrected statement only when the evidence supports its wording. If evidence remains inadequate after a targeted search, describe the exact missing record and stop rather than manufacturing a verdict.

## Review and acceptance

Test a compound claim with one supported and one unsupported component; accept separated findings. Test three articles repeating one announcement; accept one evidence origin. Test a percentage increase without the base; accept an explicit gap. Test an authoritative single controlling record; accept a narrowly supported conclusion instead of an arbitrary source-count downgrade. Record actual task outputs when exercising these cases; their presence in this reference does not certify model behavior.
