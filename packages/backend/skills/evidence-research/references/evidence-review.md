# Evidence review

## Source fitness

Evaluate a source for the proposition at issue, not through a universal ranking. Documentation can establish supported configuration but not comparative customer outcomes. A benchmark can show performance under its measured workload but not every user's environment. An internal proposal is not a final decision unless its status and authority establish that.

Inspect author/publisher, methods or provenance, date, version, incentives and directness. Prefer the primary record; use secondary material for interpretation or leads. An unavailable primary record is a gap, not permission to describe it as read.

## Independence

Trace repeated claims to their origin. Two publishers using the same survey, press release or dataset are not two independent observations of the underlying event. They may add independent interpretation; label that distinction. Independence is not established merely by different domains.

## Numbers and comparison

Before comparing percentages, identify each denominator and whether the period covers the same population. Check currency, nominal versus inflation-adjusted values, totals versus rates, and mean versus median. Do not convert missing observations to zero. Retain the original value and describe any justified conversion.

For example, two fictional plans priced at10 per seat and8 per workspace are not directly comparable without the number of seats. A performance result using a warmed cache cannot establish cold-start behavior. These are scope mismatches to resolve, not values to average.

## Conflicting records

1. Write both claims at the same level of precision.
2. Check scope, timing, versions, definitions and original evidence.
3. Identify whether one source explicitly supersedes another.
4. Seek the single missing observation or authority most likely to decide the issue.
5. If unresolved, preserve the disagreement and explain its decision impact.

Do not treat the newest timestamp, most confident writing or largest source count as a substitute for this assessment.

## Inference and confidence

Use qualitative confidence only with a reason. Direct, current evidence may strongly support a narrow claim while leaving a broader generalization uncertain. Explain limited samples, self-selection, missing controls and alternative explanations when they affect the result. Do not manufacture numeric confidence from source counts.

For a recommendation, separate the evidence from the user's priorities. An option can be best under one constraint and unsuitable under another. State which constraint drives the choice.

## Access and sensitive material

Use only authorized sources. Missing access should produce a bounded answer and an explicit gap. Do not contact people, purchase reports, upload confidential documents or retain private contents unless the task and existing permissions cover that action.

## Stop conditions

Continue when a new check could reverse a consequential conclusion. Stop when the requested issue is supported, additional results are repetitive, or the agreed effort has been reached. State what remains open; incomplete verification is a valid result when honestly bounded.
