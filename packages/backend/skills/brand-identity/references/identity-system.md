# Identity system components

## Mark and variants

Specify the primary mark/wordmark, lockups and allowed one-color/reversed variants. Test at actual required sizes and backgrounds. Derive minimum size/clear space from the asset's details and application evidence rather than copying a universal number. Include examples of distortion, unintended cropping or low-legibility placement when they prevent likely mistakes.

Retain editable vector/source and export variants appropriate to the intended workflows. Converting text to outlines can improve distribution portability while reducing editability/accessibility, so keep a suitable source and document the choice. Do not assume every logo delivery needs every format.

## Color roles

Define primary/accent, neutral and semantic roles where needed. List approved foreground/background pairings and whether a color is decorative or intended for text/controls. Light and dark applications may require different pairings, not a blanket inversion. A brand palette need not duplicate the complete product design system; define the integration boundary.

## Typography

Choose hierarchy and usage appropriate to the channel and audience. Verify font licenses, availability, fallbacks, glyph coverage and redistribution needs. Test real content, long names, numerals and relevant scripts rather than a short idealized specimen alone. A visually attractive font can fail at the actual required size or language.

## Imagery, icons and layout

Give actionable direction for subject, lighting/composition, illustration detail, icon construction, cropping and whitespace as needed. Include a few appropriate applications rather than a list of style adjectives. Keep the level of detail proportional to what another designer or operator must reproduce.

## Voice and tone

Keep a stable voice with context-sensitive tone. Describe practical wording, specificity, formality and evidence boundaries. A friendly error example must not promise saved work or support action that the product does not actually provide. Use supported product facts and genuine approved samples. Do not invent testimonials or universally require the same tone for marketing, incidents and technical documentation.
