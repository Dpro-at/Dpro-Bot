# Applications, asset packaging and rollout

## Verify real uses

Select the applications required by the brief: product UI, small icon, social graphic, slide, email, print or another actual surface. Test them with realistic content, sizes and backgrounds. Do not infer print fidelity from a monitor preview or accessibility from an attractive moodboard. State which contexts remain untested.

## Delivery structure

Identify the canonical source and current approved exports. Use clear variant/version names and include purpose, background/format and dependencies. Retain source-to-export correspondence. Test bounds, transparent backgrounds, path references and whether recipients can open the intended file type. Do not pack unrelated private assets or font files whose license does not permit redistribution.

A general guideline template is not a completed brand system. A final package should contain actual decisions/assets requested, their status and enough application guidance to use them. Keep drafts and deprecated exports distinguishable from current approved files.

## Rollout plan

Map old assets to replacements and identify owners, channels, dependencies and sequencing. Preserve product identity and public account state unless a specific authorized rollout includes them. Explain migration continuity where old and new versions must coexist. Do not replace every historical artifact merely to make a folder look consistent.

Publishing, account changes, paid assets and external sharing follow the existing task authority. Approval to design does not automatically authorize deploying a rebrand. Record actual rollout actions separately from a proposed checklist.

## Maintenance

Assign a verified owner and meaningful change trigger. Document exceptions by context and decision, rather than silently accumulating incompatible variants. A governance score can help tracking but does not prove audience preference or business lift. Keep measured results distinct from design rationale.
