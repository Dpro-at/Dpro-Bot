# Meaning and terminology

Start from what the source asserts, not a word-for-word substitution. Identify who does what, to whom, under which condition and with what degree of certainty. Preserve negation, exceptions, quantifiers, time, cause versus association and attributed opinion. Do not improve the source's argument by adding evidence or omitting a difficult qualification.

## Context and ambiguity

Read the relevant surrounding text and domain context. A term can have several meanings, and a pronoun can have an unclear referent. Resolve from supplied evidence where possible. If a material ambiguity remains, flag the alternatives or ask the narrow question that changes the output. Do not create a confident interpretation from a guessed proper name or unsupported background fact.

A typo or inconsistent source passage may need an explicit note rather than silent correction. Separate translation from source editing. If the user authorizes correction, make the meaningful change traceable. Avoid turning every minor stylistic uncertainty into a blocking question.

## Terminology

Use the confirmed project/domain glossary and relevant authoritative naming conventions. Record context for a term when one source form has different legitimate target meanings. Keep a consistent choice for the same concept, but do not force one rendering onto distinct senses. Standard names, product labels and legal/technical terms may need verification.

A bilingual dictionary is evidence about candidate senses, not automatic authority for the passage. For unfamiliar or specialized language, use trustworthy references when permitted and state unresolved uncertainty. Never invent a citation or claim native/specialist verification that did not occur.

## Register and voice

Preserve audience, formality, perspective and the intended rhetorical function. Natural target-language phrasing can require different syntax or sentence structure. Keep meaning and required document alignment intact;do not impose an absolute no-sentence-splitting rule on every language pair. If exact segment correspondence is required, follow it explicitly.

Localization or transcreation can change examples, cultural references or calls to action only within the brief. It is a separate scope choice from faithful translation. Do not add marketing promises, legal guarantees or clinical certainty while making text more persuasive.
