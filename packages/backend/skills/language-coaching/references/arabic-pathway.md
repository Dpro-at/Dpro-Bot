# Arabic pathway

Establish the learner's actual target: Modern Standard Arabic, a named spoken variety, a reading tradition or a specific exam/curriculum. The intended use determines vocabulary, pronunciation, grammar and register. Do not silently replace a requested dialect with the formal standard or treat mixed-variety speech as automatically wrong.

## Starting point

Observe script familiarity, vocabulary and the target activity separately. A heritage speaker may converse comfortably while needing reading/writing support; a formal reader may need spoken interaction practice. Choose an attainable task such as understanding a short notice, introducing oneself or asking for clarification, then teach the language that task requires.

For script learning, work from letters in words and context, including relevant joining/shape distinctions and sound-letter relationships. Use diacritics or transliteration when they support the current learner, with a plan to reduce scaffolding if reading script is the goal. Do not make transliteration compulsory at every intermediate turn or confuse romanization with actual pronunciation evidence.

## Grammar and vocabulary

Select a pattern because it helps the current task: a short nominal statement, a possession construction, a common verb phrase, negation or agreement. Scale explanation to the learner and variety. A large derivation/conjugation table can overload a beginner; a narrowly chosen contrast can clarify meaning.

Root and pattern relationships can support vocabulary, but they do not guarantee the meaning or inflection of every derived word. Verify a specific lexical form, plural or vowel pattern when uncertain. Teach common vocabulary with the relevant gender, plural or usage context where useful rather than presenting a broad rule as exception-free.

For formal grammar, distinguish sentence order, agreement, definiteness and case/register requirements in the actual example. Spoken varieties can have different patterns; do not transfer a formal ending requirement into an ordinary dialect exchange. Correct the meaningful target without turning every casual utterance into an exam on formal spelling.

## Feedback

Identify whether a form is a meaning-changing error, a variety choice, a spelling/keyboard issue or a stylistic alternative. Missing short-vowel marks in normal undiacritized writing are not automatically an error; required letters and meaning-relevant spelling may still need attention when reading/writing accuracy is the goal. Do not apply a blanket ignore-all-diacritics-and-spelling rule to every task.

Give an appropriate model in the requested variety and ask for a fresh use. Keep learner-facing examples within the task and host language policy. The maintained package itself remains English and does not contain a copied Arabic syllabus, fixed word list or upstream examples.

## Evidence

Track the skill and variety practiced. Text exchange cannot establish spoken pronunciation or comprehension. An informal impression is not a cross-variety proficiency rating. Use the learner's chosen curriculum or authoritative language references for exact forms and formal assessment requirements; no certified rating is issued here.
