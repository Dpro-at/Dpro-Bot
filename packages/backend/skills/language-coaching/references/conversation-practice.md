# Conversation practice

Agree a meaningful scenario, role, target variety/register and feedback preference. A learner ordering food, negotiating a deadline and telling a story need different vocabulary and interaction demands. Keep the scenario connected to the goal instead of asking unrelated questions indefinitely.

## Conduct the exchange

Open with a short in-role turn at a manageable level, then wait. Give the learner space to choose meaning and ask follow-up questions. Do not write both sides of the conversation unless the user requested a model dialogue. Adjust the complexity from observed performance rather than assuming that longer sentences are always better.

For fluency practice, preserve the exchange and reserve noncritical corrections for a pause or brief end review. For accuracy practice, make the selected target visible and offer timely focused feedback. Respect the learner's preference to pause, change topic or stop. Tutoring does not become a permanent overlay on unrelated work without the user's request.

## Repair and ambiguity

If meaning is unclear, use an appropriate conversational clarification before rewriting the entire response. Offer a hint, alternative phrase or short model when useful. Distinguish understood-but-awkward from genuinely ambiguous. A valid regional expression may differ from a textbook standard; identify variety/register rather than calling it universally incorrect.

Avoid stereotypes or invented cultural rules. Explain context-dependent politeness with appropriate uncertainty and verified references when needed. Sensitive personal details are not necessary to practice a role; use fictional scenarios and let the learner choose comfortable content.

## Modality limits

In text, assess the written exchange and interaction choices. Do not claim to hear pronunciation, timing, accent or listening ability. With actual authorized audio capability, record what audio was available and consider recognition uncertainty before correcting the learner. A speech-to-text transcription error is not automatically a pronunciation error.

## Debrief

Return a small set of useful observations: communication achieved, one recurring obstacle and a reusable phrase or strategy. Invite a short replay or a new situation that checks transfer. Record independent versus assisted performance. Avoid an arbitrary score or praise for fluency without evidence.
