# User research

Reduce uncertainty about a real product/user decision through an appropriate study and traceable evidence. A plausible persona, an invented interview or an attractive affinity map is not evidence from participants.

Read [Runtime contract](references/runtime-contract.md) in a new host. Use [Research design](references/research-design.md), [Sessions and usability](references/sessions-usability.md), [Synthesis and inference](references/synthesis-inference.md), [Research plan](assets/research-plan.md), [Session guide](assets/session-guide.md), [Evidence and findings](assets/evidence-findings.md) and [Acceptance](references/acceptance.md). Keep participant data in the authorized protected location, not in a shared skill package.

## Start from the decision

Identify the decision owner, product context, intended audience, current uncertainty, what evidence could change the decision and practical constraints. Inspect existing research, analytics, support evidence and known product behavior before proposing another study. Separate established findings from hypotheses and stakeholder beliefs.

Choose whether the task is a plan, guide, supplied-evidence synthesis, actual study execution or evaluation of a specific flow. A request to write a plan does not authorize recruitment, contacting people, incentives, recordings or external uploads. Complete the authorized independent work and identify exact remaining actions without manufacturing participant evidence.

## Choose a method that answers the question

Read [Research design](references/research-design.md). Match exploratory interviews, contextual observation, usability tasks, survey, diary, card sorting or experiment to the actual uncertainty. Explain what the method can and cannot establish. A few qualitative sessions can reveal concrete usability failures without supporting a population prevalence estimate.

Define participant relevance, inclusion/exclusion, context and sampling limitations. Do not select only convenient favorable users and call the sample representative. A proposed sample size should follow the question, diversity, risk and practical evidence needs; no fixed universal count certifies saturation or statistical validity.

Predefine the useful observations or measures for the study. In a usability task, establish success, failure, assistance and the relevant starting state. For a survey, define constructs, neutral wording, response choices and how sampling/nonresponse can affect interpretation. An online poll is not automatically a representative experiment.

## Prepare truthful study material

Use [Session guide](assets/session-guide.md). Write neutral prompts and realistic tasks that do not reveal the intended solution. Test the guide/prototype with a bounded rehearsal when possible. Keep product claims and task data accurate; label hypothetical scenarios and sample data.

Plan the actual permission/consent and data-handling process for participants, including recording and sharing boundaries where relevant. Use the organization's approved process and applicable authoritative requirements rather than inventing a universal legal policy. Collect only what the authorized study needs and keep identity mapping separate from ordinary synthesis artifacts.

## Conduct only the authorized study

Read [Sessions and usability](references/sessions-usability.md). Establish participant understanding, task context and relevant recording scope. Observe behavior, use neutral probes and distinguish spontaneous action from assisted completion. Do not teach the solution and then count success as unassisted usability.

Record what actually occurred using stable protected evidence references. Keep participant statements, observed behavior, researcher interpretation and proposed design responses separate. A quote must come from inspected evidence with appropriate sharing permission; do not invent or polish it into a statement the participant did not make.

If only supplied notes are available, synthesize those notes with their limits. Missing recordings, unclear dates or an unknown sampling process should remain explicit. A simulated critique or expert walkthrough can help independent evaluation but cannot be reported as real participant research.

## Synthesize with traceability

Use [Synthesis and inference](references/synthesis-inference.md). Code relevant observations, group meaningful patterns and retain disconfirming cases. Preserve the link from each finding to evidence and the decision it affects. Do not let a polished theme erase important contradictions or turn one memorable quote into a universal user need.

For numeric measures, retain the denominator, task conditions, sample and assistance rules. Qualitative frequency in a convenience sample does not establish population prevalence. Separate observation, finding, interpretation, recommendation and unresolved hypothesis.

## Deliver a decision and useful next step

Lead with what the evidence supports for the actual decision, major limitations and concrete implications. Prioritize by user impact, evidence strength and relevant consequence rather than fabricated impact scores. A high-severity issue can arise from a reproducible blocker even when a small study cannot estimate its population frequency.

Deliver the requested plan, guide, findings, protected evidence map and next useful study or design check. State whether recruitment, sessions, transcription, synthesis and implementation actually occurred. Do not claim user validation because a research document exists or a stakeholder approved a prototype.

Maintain ownership, study/version and review trigger. Keep private participant data, incentives, recordings and internal product materials out of general portable packages. Research guidance does not grant outreach, account access, data export or publishing authority.

[Design notes](references/design-notes.md) identify reviewed material and comparison boundaries.
