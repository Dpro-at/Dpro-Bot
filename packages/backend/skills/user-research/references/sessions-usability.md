# Sessions, observations and usability

## Prepare

Verify the actual prototype/build, task starting state, fixtures, access and known limitations. Label nonfunctional prototypes so an absent behavior is not incorrectly attributed to user confusion. Define success/failure/assistance and relevant timing rules before observation where feasible.

Check participant understanding and the actual consent/recording scope through the approved process. Do not expose private data or another user's account to make a scenario realistic. A hypothetical scenario should be labeled, not presented as an actual customer record.

## Facilitate neutrally

Let the participant act before explaining. Ask about their expectations and reasoning without implying the desired answer. Keep encouragement distinct from instruction. If assistance is necessary, record what changed and do not count the result as unassisted success.

Distinguish spontaneous behavior, prompted response and researcher interpretation. A participant saying an interface is easy can coexist with a failed task. Retain that contrast rather than selecting only the favorable statement.

## Evidence record

Use stable participant/session/task identifiers and protected source locations. Capture the relevant observation, context and assistance. Keep identity mapping and sensitive raw material out of ordinary synthesis. Pseudonyms do not automatically make rich contextual material anonymous; follow actual sharing permissions.

Quotes should preserve meaning and be traceable to inspected evidence. Redaction can be necessary, but do not rewrite a participant's statement into a stronger endorsement or claim. If only notes exist, identify their completeness and uncertainty.

## Remote and asynchronous work

Tool completion or an uploaded recording is not proof of the intended study conditions. Inspect the task/version, actual session and available evidence before counting it. Technical failure, abandonment, missing data and genuine task failure should remain distinguishable.

## Debrief

Ask useful clarifying questions without pressuring participants to validate the concept. Record issues that need follow-up and keep research results separate from promises to change the product. No outreach, publication or participant-data export occurs merely because this procedure describes it.
