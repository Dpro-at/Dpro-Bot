# Research design and method boundaries

## Decision and evidence

Write the decision, uncertainty and what could change it. A method is useful when its evidence can discriminate among plausible explanations or design choices. Avoid conducting interviews simply because they are familiar or asking people for preferences when the real issue is whether they can complete a task.

| Method | Useful evidence | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews | Context, motivations, recalled workflow and language | Self-report is not direct behavioral observation |
| Contextual observation | Actual practices and environmental constraints | Limited settings/time can miss other contexts |
| Usability tasks | Concrete success/failure and interaction problems | Small purposive samples do not estimate universal prevalence |
| Survey | Responses under a defined instrument/sample | Wording, nonresponse and selection shape interpretation |
| Diary | Experiences over time in participants' context | Compliance and retrospective reporting vary |
| Card sorting/tree tasks | Organization/label expectations and navigation outcomes | Method setup affects what the result establishes |
| Controlled experiment | Comparative effects under a defensible design | Requires valid assignment/measurement and relevant inference |

## Sampling

Define the intended population and the actual recruitment route. Identify relevant experience, context and diversity rather than a generic demographic persona. Record exclusions and inaccessible groups. Convenience participants can still reveal useful problems, but do not call them representative without support.

Plan the study size and stopping rule around the actual question, heterogeneity, consequence and available resources. A fixed count does not prove saturation. Quantitative precision needs the relevant design assumptions and independent units; a qualitative count is not a substitute.

## Instrument design

Use neutral wording, balanced response choices and realistic scenarios. Avoid asking two constructs in one question or framing a preference with an unsupported benefit. In usability tasks, specify a goal without revealing the control sequence being tested. Pilot the instrument to detect ambiguous wording or a broken prototype.

## Scope and participant handling

Separate planning from authorized recruitment/session execution. Use approved consent/recording/sharing processes and minimize personal data. Incentives and external tools may have cost or account implications. A research skill does not authorize those actions. Keep unresolved process decisions explicit instead of inventing approvals.
